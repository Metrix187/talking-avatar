Talking Avatar

Turn one portrait into a face you can talk to. You speak or type, an LLM answers, a local voice reads the answer, and a diffusion model animates the portrait in step with the voice, streamed to a browser tab as it is generated.

Everything except the LLM runs on your own machine. Built and measured on an RTX 5070 Laptop GPU with 8 GB of memory.

Code and setup on GitHub The model: SoulX-FlashHead
This page is a recording, not a live demo. The app needs a GPU with about 6 GB of memory, so it runs locally rather than in this Space.

Animated characters too

Drawn and 3D-animated characters keep their style. These three were generated for this project and animated by the same app, on the same laptop.

  • A clearly drawn mouth lip-syncs well. A mouth that is a thin line, or hidden under a moustache, barely moves.
  • Faces soften while speaking, and some characters soften much more than others.
  • Flat cartoons often have no face the detector recognises; the picture is then used as it is.

How it works

  1. Hearfaster-whisper turns your speech into text, on the CPU.
  2. ThinkAn LLM streams a reply (Meta Model API or Claude).
  3. SpeakKokoro voices each sentence as it arrives, on the CPU.
  4. AnimateSoulX-FlashHead Lite draws 24 frames for every 0.96 s of speech.
  5. StreamFrames and audio travel together and play on one clock, so lips and voice stay locked.

Limits

  • Head and shoulders only, 512 x 512. No body, hands or camera motion.
  • Replies start after a second or two, not instantly: each chunk has to be rendered before it can play.
  • On a laptop the GPU has to be kept busy between chunks or its clock drops and the video falls behind, which costs about 108 W while the avatar is on screen.
  • The FlashHead weights carry no clear licence text, so this is for tinkering. Only animate faces you have the right to use.
